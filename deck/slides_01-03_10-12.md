## Slide 2: Executive summary

**Headline:** HSIC lost a third of its share of MetroMart spend, evenly across every customer, so the fix is the product,
not a target list

**Three findings**
1. **The decline is real and comes from cards that stayed open.** SoW fell **28.9% → 19.5%** (FY25 → FY26), and the
   continuously open cards explain **−9.54 of the −9.4 points** (`f1_sow_fy25`, `f1_sow_fy26`, `f2_stayer_pp`). Customers
   didn't shop less: MetroMart spend per card was flat (**₹1,037 → ₹1,029**) while HSIC spend per card more than halved
   (**₹356 → ₹167**; `f14_*`). **M**
2. **It is universal.** Every customer type fell at the same rate, and customers don't differ in how often they reach for
   the card (variance ratio **1.00**; year-over-year r **≈ 0**; `x1_*`). So "low SoW" is the whole book. Cash/UPI took
   **47%** of the lost share (`f3_cash_upi_share_of_loss`). **M**
3. **Closures doubled and sit in young cards.** **1.7% → 3.4%** of open cards close each quarter (`f9_rate_first`,
   `f9_rate_last`); cards under 6 months close at **14.7%** in six months, **4.8×** cards over 3 years (`f10_2026_01_31_*`). **M**

**What it's worth:** **₹33.4M** of HSIC spend lost in FY26 at constant SoW (`f12_gap`) **M**. Net of the **1.40%** HSIC pays
in rewards (`f15_reward_rate_fy26`) **M**, that spend carried only **₹0.03–0.20M** of margin, or **₹0.27–0.43M** if MetroMart
co-funds half the rewards (`f15_net_margin_*`), at an assumed 1.5–2% interchange **A**. The stake is retention and
revolving balances, not interchange.

**Three actions**
1. **Show the reward at the till (O3).** No incentive cost. It pays back from the first rupee moved on 1% baskets in every
   scenario (`o3_other_breakeven_*`) **S**; on Prime Grocery + Electronics it pays back in the base and optimistic cases but
   never in the conservative case (`o3_breakeven_*`) **S**, which is why we ask MetroMart to co-fund those rewards.
2. **Match Cash/UPI's immediacy: instant cashback and the card on UPI (O1, O2).** Why Cash/UPI wins is a hypothesis
   (immediacy) that the pilot tests **H**.
3. **Protect young cards, win back closed cardholders (O7, O6), prioritised with the frequency × card-age 2×2**, and pilot
   everything with 10% holdouts.

**Speaker notes (60 s):** The share fell by a third in a year, and it isn't a group of customers drifting away: it's
everyone, at the same rate, while they keep shopping at MetroMart as much as before. That changes the answer. A target list
of "at-risk" customers would be chosen at random, so the first fixes are product fixes that reach every basket. We price
every offer net of the rewards HSIC already pays, because that margin is thin, and we show which ones never pay back.

## Slide 3: Data, rules and assumptions

**Headline:** Built to the brief's rules, checked twice, every number labelled

**Left: the brief's rules, implemented**
- SoW = HSIC net spend ÷ total net spend at MetroMart, as a ratio of sums, only while the card is open (open and close
  dates inclusive).
- Net sales: returns subtract; returns count as 0 transactions.
- MetroMart fiscal year, Aug–Jul: FY25 = Aug 2024–Jul 2025, FY26 = Aug 2025–Jul 2026.
- Categories and payment methods from the lookup tables; HSIC = payment code 3.
- Rewards: Prime 5% Grocery, 3% Electronics, 1% other; Non-Prime 1%; credited the following month.

**Middle: the data**
- 45,000 customers, of whom 38,164 hold a card (`f15_cardholders`); 6,836 have never held one and are left out of SoW.
- 444,118 transactions, 1 Aug 2024 – 31 Jul 2026; 44,814 returns.
- **Checks:** 39 reproduction checks pass; two independent builds of the core numbers agree exactly.

**Right: label legend and key assumptions**
- **M Measured** from the data · **S Simulated** (an offer's result under stated inputs) · **A Assumed** (an input the
  data doesn't contain) · **H Hypothesis** (a "why" the pilot will test).
- **A** Amounts in ₹; Travel and Bill Payments earn the 1% "other eligible" rate; a return reduces the payment method on
  its own row.
- **A** Interchange 1.5 / 1.75 / 2%; revolve income 0 / 1 / 2% of spend; MetroMart co-funding of rewards 0 / 50 / 50%
  (conservative / base / optimistic). The reward rate (1.40%) is measured, not assumed.

**Speaker notes:** One sentence each on the rules; the point is that every figure on the following slides carries its
label, so you can see at a glance what we measured and what we assumed.

## Slide 10: Offers

**Headline:** Three product fixes first, because they reach every basket

**Charts:** none (offer cards); `11_breakeven.png` sits on slide 11.

**The three offers**
| | Offer | Why | What it costs | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **O3 (hero)** | **Reward visibility:** a till and receipt line, "Pay with HSIC: earn ₹X on this basket", and a monthly "₹ you left on the table" | No rival card matches HSIC's Grocery and Electronics rates, yet Prime members use HSIC exactly like Non-Prime members and left **₹1.60M** of cashback on the table in FY26 (`f8_prime_ge_left_on_table`) **M** | No incentive. The shifted spend earns the customer's normal reward | **H-aware:** Prime members don't notice their rates **H** |
| **O1** | **Instant cashback at the till:** the same reward, credited at checkout instead of on next month's statement | Cash/UPI took **47%** of the lost share, in every ticket band (`f3_cash_upi_share_of_loss`) **M** | One month of funding on the reward (assumed 8% a year, `assumed_cost_of_funds`) **A**: negligible | **H-imm:** customers choose Cash/UPI for immediacy **H** |
| **O2** | **HSIC card on UPI** | Meets customers where the spend went; FY26 Cash/UPI spend by cardholders: **₹82.8M** (`econin_o2_eligible`) **M** | Partnership and network costs, not in the data | — |

**One line each (detail in the appendix):**
- **O4 No-cost EMI on ₹3,000+ baskets:** if every ₹3k+ HSIC basket took it, **69%** of eligible spend would have to move to
  pay back (base, `o4_breakeven_base`) **S**, so offer it targeted and co-funded, not blanket.
- **O5 Small-basket streak:** *pending redesign* — no cardholder made 5 small HSIC purchases in any FY26 month (max **3**,
  `o5_max_small_hsic_purchases_in_a_month`) **M**.
- **O6 Win-back for 5,903 closed cardholders still shopping:** pays back from the first rupee recovered in the base and
  optimistic cases, never in the conservative case (`o6_breakeven_*`) **S**.
- **O7 Young-card protection:** the 3-month Prime trial pays back if it keeps **4.0%** of trial cards open (base,
  `o7_breakeven_saved_base`) **S**; *milestone pending redesign* (3 swipes in 60 days: **0.1%** of new cards; first use in 60 days:
  **15%**, `o7_milestone_reach`, `o7_first_use_60d_share`) **M**.
- **O8 Threshold bonus (segment D):** **91%** of D's card spend would earn it with no change in behaviour, because spend is
  lumpy (`econin_o8_noise_share`) **M**; needs **8%** of eligible spend to move (base) **S**.

**Speaker notes (60 s):** Lead with O3 because it is the cheapest lever we have: HSIC already pays the best Grocery and
Electronics rates in the market, and Prime members still don't use the card more. Making the reward visible costs nothing
extra; whether it works is exactly what the pilot tests. O1 and O2 go after the Cash/UPI habit. The paid offers come
after, and only where their economics hold.

## Slide 11: Economics and pilot

**Headline:** The margin on moved spend is thin, so the cheapest offers lead and everything is piloted

**Charts:** `11_net_margin_waterfall.png` (left) · `11_breakeven.png` (right)

**Points**
- **What HSIC keeps on ₹100 moved to the card (base):** **₹2.05** on general spend vs **₹0.25** on Prime grocery
  (`econ_margin_general_base`, `econ_margin_prime_grocery_base`) **S**. Conservative: **+₹0.10** and **−₹3.50** **S**.
  Inputs: interchange, revolve income and co-funding are **A**; the 1.40% reward rate is **M**.
- **Break-even shift** (share of an offer's eligible non-HSIC spend that must move for it to pay back): **0%** for
  reward visibility on 1% baskets, the card on UPI and win-back in the base case; **never** for the paid offers in the
  conservative case (`o*_breakeven_*`) **S**. Operating costs aren't in the data, so each point is a floor.
- **Each 1% of eligible spend moved is worth:** **₹53k a year** for reward visibility on 1% baskets, **₹55k** for instant
  cashback, **₹17k** for the card on UPI (base, `o3_other_value_per_1pct_base`, `o1_value_per_1pct_base`,
  `o2_value_per_1pct_base`) **S**. Per 10,000 cardholders: divide by 3.8.
- **Pilot:** a randomised **10% holdout** per offer and segment, read after 3 months on SoW, active rate, closure rate and
  net value. Across the grid it can detect a **1.8-point** change in HSIC share (`mde_grid`); a single box only **2.6–6.1
  points** (`mde_E`, `mde_B`), so read the pilot at grid level or run it for 6 months **S**. **O3 vs holdout tests
  H-aware; O1 vs holdout tests H-imm.** Scale only if the lift's CI excludes 0 and net value > 0.

**Speaker notes (60 s):** Rewards eat most of the interchange, so a rupee moved to the card is worth about two paise in the
base case and can lose money on Prime grocery. That's why reward visibility leads: it adds no cost. Every paid offer shows
the shift it needs to pay back, and we say "never" where it can't. Nothing here is a forecast of recovery; the pilot is what
turns these simulations into measured results.

**If a judge asks**
| Question | Answer (key) |
|---|---|
| Why not lead with the ₹33M? | It's spend, not profit: net of rewards it carried ₹0.03–0.43M of margin (`f15_net_margin_*`) |
| What makes O3 profitable on Prime Grocery + Electronics? | Co-funding. With MetroMart paying half the rewards it pays back at base interchange with no revolve income at all; with no co-funding it needs revolve income of about 1.6% of spend (`o3_breakeven_revolve`, `o3_breakeven_revolve_nocofund`) |
| What revolve income do the offers need? | Shown per offer as the break-even revolve rate, with base co-funding and with none (`o*_breakeven_revolve`, `o*_breakeven_revolve_nocofund`) |
| Why is the pilot so long? | Each purchase is close to a coin flip with p ≈ 0.16, and a card makes about 1.5 purchases a quarter, so effects under ~2 points need the whole grid |

## Slide 12: Roadmap and asks

**Headline:** Three decisions this quarter, one pilot, one ask of MetroMart

**Decisions for HSIC**
1. Ship reward visibility (O3) at the till and on the statement: product work only, no incentive budget.
2. Pilot instant cashback (O1) against a 10% holdout; start the UPI-linkage track (O2).
3. Adopt the card-age rule for retention: protect cards in their first year (O7), and offer win-back to closed cardholders
   who still shop (O6).

**The ask of MetroMart**
- **Co-fund Prime category rewards** (grocery and electronics). At half the reward cost, reward visibility on those baskets
  pays back at base interchange without any revolving income; without it, it needs about 1.6% of spend in revolve income.
  Co-funding also lowers every paid offer's break-even.
- Co-fund no-cost EMI subvention on big baskets (O4) for targeted use.

**Pilot timeline:** month 0 launch with holdouts → month 3 first read (grid level) → month 6 decision per offer.
